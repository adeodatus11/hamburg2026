The experience of COVE Poland within the WIN4SMEs project

  • Vocational education as a shared responsibility
  • From separate institutions to practical cooperation
  • Making VET more attractive, visible and useful
Good morning. My name is Maciej Najwer and I represent COVE Poland and Vocational School Complex No. 5 in Wrocław. In this short presentation, I would like to share our experience of building cooperation around vocational education in Poland. Our main goal is clear: we want to strengthen vocational education and make it more attractive for young people, parents, teachers, employers and local institutions. But the path to this goal was not obvious from the beginning.

A Difficult but Promising Context

Different institutions — one shared goal

  • Vocational school
  • Chamber of Crafts
  • University
  • Employers and local partners
  • One goal — but different daily realities
When we started as the Polish COVE team, we had different institutions around one table. We had a vocational school, the Chamber of Crafts, Wrocław University of Science and Technology, and other partners connected with education and the labour market. At the beginning, it was not completely clear how we should cooperate in practice. We shared the same general goal — strengthening vocational education — but we came from different realities. A school thinks about students, lessons and teachers. A craft chamber thinks about employers, qualifications and apprenticeships. A university thinks about research, innovation and knowledge transfer. So our first challenge was not only to implement an activity plan. Our first challenge was to find a common language.

Over time, we discovered something important. The fact that we are different is not a weakness. It is our strength. We do not need to duplicate each other's work. Instead, we can share what each of us already has. The school brings direct contact with students, teachers and everyday practice. The craft sector brings the perspective of employers and real work. The university brings innovation, research and new methods. The project gives us a structure and a reason to connect these perspectives. This is where cooperation becomes real — not as a formal declaration, but as a practical exchange of knowledge.

Making VET More Attractive

  • Better perception of vocational education
  • Stronger communication with students and parents
  • Practical skills and real career paths
  • Closer cooperation with employers
  • VET as a modern and ambitious choice
VET should not be seen as a second choice.
It is a modern, practical and ambitious route.
The central idea behind our work is to make vocational education more attractive. This does not mean only better promotion or nicer visual materials. It means showing the real value of vocational education: practical skills, employability, entrepreneurship, independence and future development. In Poland, vocational education still needs a stronger and more positive narrative. Students and parents should not see VET as a second-choice path. They should see it as a modern, practical and ambitious route.

From Project Tasks to a Wider Mission

We implement the activity plan

  • Meetings and workshops
  • Study visits
  • Reporting and documentation
  • Partner coordination

But we want to go further

  • Results must be visible
  • Materials must be easy to access
  • Impact requires communication
  • Usability is part of sustainability
Of course, we are implementing the activity plan. This is necessary and important. But for us, the project is not only about completing tasks. We want to go further. We want our work to be visible, understandable and useful for people outside the immediate project team. If we create valuable materials, but they remain hidden in long documents or difficult-to-access folders, their real impact is limited. That is why we treat communication, accessibility and visual clarity as part of the project's sustainability.

Making Results Easy to Find and Use

  • Clear online presence from the beginning
  • Practical formats, not only long documents
  • Materials for teachers, schools, employers and partners
  • Dissemination means making results useful — not just reporting them
From the beginning, we wanted COVE Poland to be visible online. This is not only about promotion. It is about making our results easier to find and easier to use. Long PDF documents are important. They are detailed and precise. But they are not always the easiest format for everyday use. So we are building websites, thematic subpages and practical materials that can be opened, read, translated and used by different groups. For us, dissemination is not only reporting what we have done. Dissemination means making project results useful for others.

Creating Spaces Where Cooperation Can Happen

Shared workshops & meetings
Study visits & joint training
Common materials & tools
Relationships first — systems second
One of our main conclusions is that cooperation needs real spaces. It is not enough to say that schools and employers should cooperate. We need practical formats: meetings, workshops, study visits, joint training, shared materials and common projects. This is why we focus on networking. We want to connect vocational schools with employers, universities, chambers of crafts and local institutions. The goal is to build relationships first, and then build systems on top of these relationships.

AI should support teachers — not replace them.
Responsible use, in cooperation with higher education.
One of our practical pilot areas is artificial intelligence. But we do not treat AI as the goal itself. AI is only useful if it supports real educational work. We are interested in practical applications: lesson planning, preparing materials, supporting communication, analysing documents and helping teachers organise their work. Cooperation with higher education is very important here, because it helps us connect innovation with responsible implementation. The key message is simple: AI should support teachers, not replace them.

Another important area is teacher onboarding. In Poland, we already have formal roles: the novice teacher and the mentor. But formal roles do not automatically create a good process. Many schools still need practical tools that answer simple questions: What should happen in the first week? What should the mentor and novice teacher discuss? What documents and routines should be explained? How should school knowledge be transferred between generations of teachers? This is why we are developing a structured onboarding process. It is meant to support the novice teacher, the mentor and the school leadership. For us, onboarding is not only an internal HR topic. It is part of strengthening vocational education, because strong schools need supported teachers.

At first, these topics may look different: networks, promotion, AI, onboarding, websites. But for us, they are connected by one logic: transfer of knowledge. We want knowledge to move between institutions. From university to school. From employers to education. From experienced teachers to novice teachers. From project documents to practical tools. From isolated actions to shared capacity. This is the real value of the project for us.

What We Are Learning

  • Cooperation often starts without obvious common ground
  • Shared spaces are discovered through practical action
  • VET needs visibility, accessibility and strong partnerships
  • Small pilots can fill real systemic gaps
  • The project can become a platform for further development
We are not only implementing an activity plan.
We are building shared spaces where vocational education can become more attractive, practical and visible.
To conclude, our experience is that cooperation does not always start with a ready-made common space. Sometimes this space has to be discovered. At the beginning, we had different institutions around one table. Over time, we learned that each partner brings something valuable. The activity plan gives us structure. But the wider mission is to make vocational education stronger, more attractive and more visible. For us, this means building networks, communicating clearly, creating accessible materials, testing AI tools and developing practical solutions such as teacher onboarding. Our goal is not only to complete project tasks. Our goal is to create solutions that can be used, adapted and developed further.
